%% ── ABSTRACT ────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Deep neural networks produce overconfident predictions on
inputs far outside their training distribution---a critical
failure in safety-sensitive domains.
We address out-of-distribution (OOD) detection by moving
the energy-based scoring function from a discriminative
classifier into the discrete latent space of a
Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) trained
exclusively on in-distribution data.
An unsupervised energy model trained on VQ-VAE latent codes
with a contrastive hinge loss requires no OOD labels.
A supervised MLP in the same latent space provides an
upper-bound comparison.
On CIFAR-10 (in-distribution), CIFAR-100 (near-OOD), and
SVHN (far-OOD), our Latent MLP achieves AUROC~0.980 on
SVHN---surpassing the energy baseline by 4.6 points and
reducing FPR@95 from 25.8\% to 10.0\%.
On the harder near-OOD task the energy baseline retains a
15-point AUROC advantage, motivating richer pseudo-OOD
strategies as future work.
Code: \url{https://github.com/harshitjain25/csc8851-vqvae-ood}.
\end{abstract}

%% ── 1. INTRODUCTION ─────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}

Deep neural networks achieve remarkable accuracy on standard
benchmarks, yet they suffer from a systematic overconfidence
problem: they assign high-confidence predictions to inputs
that bear no resemblance to their training data~\cite{nguyen2015}.
A classifier trained on CIFAR-10 objects may confidently
label a street-view digit as ``automobile,'' or return a
confident class label for pure Gaussian noise.
In autonomous driving, clinical decision-support, and
industrial inspection, such silent failures are
unacceptable---making out-of-distribution (OOD) detection a
critical capability for safe deployment.

\textbf{Problem formulation.}
Let $p_{\mathrm{in}}(x)$ be the training distribution.
At inference, given input $x$, we decide whether
$x\!\sim\!p_{\mathrm{in}}$ or $x\!\sim\!p_{\mathrm{out}}$
via a scoring function $s:\mathcal{X}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}$
and threshold $\tau$:
\begin{equation}
  g(x;\tau) = \mathbf{1}[s(x)>\tau].   \label{eq:decision}
\end{equation}
The goal is to choose $s$ so that in-distribution inputs
score low and OOD inputs score high.

\textbf{Limitations of existing methods.}
The maximum softmax probability~\cite{hendrycks2017} is a
popular baseline but notoriously overconfident: softmax
normalisation forces probability mass onto a known class
even for unfamiliar inputs.
Liu~\etal~\cite{liu2020} showed that the free energy
derived from raw classifier logits,
\begin{equation}
  E(x;f) = -T\cdot\log\!\sum_{i=1}^{K} e^{f_i(x)/T},
  \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
is better calibrated and correlates with the log-partition
function of the data distribution.
Yet this energy originates from a classifier optimised for
\emph{class separability}: it measures how well an input
fits the known classes, not whether it resembles the
overall visual structure of in-distribution images.

\textbf{Our approach.}
We hypothesise that moving the energy surface into a
\emph{generative} latent space better captures
distributional familiarity.
A VQ-VAE~\cite{vqvae2017} trained solely on CIFAR-10 builds
a discrete visual vocabulary from reconstruction loss
alone; OOD images forced through the same encoder produce
latent codes that ``look unfamiliar'' because the codebook
was never exposed to those visual patterns.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
  \item We faithfully reproduce the WRN-40-2 energy
        detector of Liu~\etal~\cite{liu2020} and confirm
        their reported numbers on CIFAR-100 and SVHN.
  \item We train an \emph{unsupervised} latent energy model
        on VQ-VAE embeddings via a contrastive hinge loss,
        requiring no OOD labels.
  \item We train a supervised latent MLP as an upper-bound
        comparison and acknowledge its data-leakage
        limitation.
  \item We show that a grid-searched blend weight
        $\alpha^*$ correctly discards a near-random signal,
        preventing it from contaminating a strong detector.
\end{enumerate}

%% ── 2. RELATED WORK ─────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Softmax confidence.}
Hendrycks and Gimpel~\cite{hendrycks2017} proposed the
maximum softmax probability as an OOD score.
While it serves as a strong initial baseline, the
normalisation constraint makes scores unreliable for inputs
that differ substantially from the training manifold.

\textbf{ODIN.}
Liang~\etal~\cite{liang2018} widen the in/OOD gap using
temperature scaling and gradient-based input perturbations.
Both hyperparameters must be re-tuned for each OOD dataset,
limiting generalisation at deployment.

\textbf{Mahalanobis distance.}
Lee~\etal~\cite{lee2018} fit class-conditional Gaussians to
penultimate-layer features and use Mahalanobis distance as
the OOD score.
The approach is effective but assumes Gaussian features and
requires storing per-class statistics.

\textbf{Outlier Exposure.}
Hendrycks~\etal~\cite{hendrycks2019} showed that
fine-tuning a classifier on an auxiliary OOD dataset can
significantly improve detection.
Our supervised latent MLP is inspired by this idea but
applies it in VQ-VAE latent space and inherits the same
risk: if the auxiliary distribution does not match real
test OOD data, the model overfits the proxy.

\textbf{Energy-based OOD.}
Liu~\etal~\cite{liu2020} provided the theoretical grounding
for Eq.~\eqref{eq:energy} as an OOD score, showing it
avoids the overconfidence bias of softmax.
Our work retains this energy formulation but relocates it
from a discriminative classifier to a generative latent
space, replacing class-level features with
reconstruction-driven features.

\textbf{VQ-VAE.}
Van~den~Oord~\etal~\cite{vqvae2017} introduced the
Vector-Quantized VAE, which encodes images as sequences of
discrete tokens from a learned codebook.
Because the codebook is shaped entirely by the
reconstruction objective on in-distribution data, it
provides a signal for distributional familiarity orthogonal
to any class label information.

\subsection{VQ-VAE Latent Encoding}

The VQ-VAE (trained by Parsh Jadon) maps each $32{\times}32$
image through encoder $E_\theta$, codebook
$\mathcal{C}=\{\mathbf{e}_k\}_{k=1}^{K}$ ($K\!=\!512$), and
decoder $D_\phi$.
The continuous embedding
$\mathbf{z}_e\!=\!E_\theta(x)\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{8\times8\times64}$
is quantised to the nearest entry:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z}_q = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{e}_k\in\mathcal{C}}
    \|\mathbf{z}_e-\mathbf{e}_k\|_2.   \label{eq:quant}
\end{equation}
Training minimises the three-term objective
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vq}} =
  \underbrace{\|x{-}D_\phi(\mathbf{z}_q)\|^2}_{\text{recon.}}
  +\underbrace{\|\mathrm{sg}[\mathbf{z}_e]{-}\mathbf{e}_k\|^2}_{\text{codebook}}
  +\beta\underbrace{\|\mathbf{z}_e{-}\mathrm{sg}[\mathbf{e}_k]\|^2}_{\text{commit.}},
  \label{eq:vqloss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{sg}[\cdot]$ is stop-gradient and $\beta\!=\!0.25$.
The model trains with Adam (lr\,$=10^{-3}$), batch 128, for
10 epochs on the CIFAR-10 training set (50,000 images).
Because the codebook is shaped solely by CIFAR-10
reconstruction, it encodes only in-distribution visual
patterns.
Flattening the quantised tensor yields
$\mathbf{z}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{4096}$.

\subsection{Latent Energy Model}

$E_{\mathrm{lat}}$ is a three-layer MLP (256 hidden units,
ReLU) mapping $\mathbb{R}^{4096}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}$.
It is trained \emph{only} on CIFAR-10 latents; Gaussian
noise $\mathbf{z}_n\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$
acts as cheap pseudo-OOD, requiring no real OOD data.
A squared hinge loss with margin targets
$m_{\mathrm{in}}\!=\!-10$ and $m_{\mathrm{out}}\!=\!-5$
enforces separation:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{in}} &=
    \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}\sim p_\mathrm{ID}}\!
    [\max(0,E_{\mathrm{lat}}(\mathbf{z})-m_{\mathrm{in}})^2],
    \label{eq:lin}\\
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{out}} &=
    \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}\sim\mathcal{N}}\!
    [\max(0,m_{\mathrm{out}}-E_{\mathrm{lat}}(\mathbf{z}))^2],
    \label{eq:lout}
\end{align}
with total loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{energy}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{in}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{out}}$.
The hinge zeroes the gradient once a sample is already on
the correct side of its margin, preventing energy collapse.
Training uses Adam (lr\,$=10^{-3}$) for 10 epochs.

\subsection{Supervised OOD MLP}

For an upper-bound comparison we train a binary MLP with the
same architecture, optimised with binary cross-entropy:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MLP}} = -\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{z},y)}\!
  [y\log\hat{y}+(1{-}y)\log(1{-}\hat{y})],
  \label{eq:bce}
\end{equation}
where $y\!=\!0$ for CIFAR-10 latents and $y\!=\!1$ for
CIFAR-100 / SVHN latents.

\noindent\textbf{Data-leakage caveat.}
We found that this setup constitutes \emph{data leakage}:
the supervised MLP uses labeled samples from the same
datasets evaluated at test time.
A principled design would train on a small labeled set from
a \emph{disjoint} auxiliary source and evaluate zero-shot.
We retain this setting to measure an upper bound, flagging
it as a limitation in Section~\ref{sec:conclusions}.

\subsection{Combined Score and Threshold}

Both scores are min-max normalised to $[0,1]$ using
CIFAR-10 statistics:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{s} = (s-s_{\min})\,/\,(s_{\max}-s_{\min}).
  \label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}
The combined detection score is a weighted average,
\begin{equation}
  s_{\mathrm{comb}}(\mathbf{z}) =
    \alpha\,\tilde{E}_{\mathrm{lat}}(\mathbf{z})
    +(1{-}\alpha)\,\tilde{y}(\mathbf{z}),
  \label{eq:comb}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha^*$ is grid-searched over
$\{0.0,0.1,\ldots,1.0\}$ on a held-out validation split.
The detection threshold $\tau$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:decision}
is fixed so that TPR\,=\,0.95 on the in-distribution test
set.

\subsection{Implementation Challenges}

\noindent\textbf{Memory-mapped OOD data.}
Loading all 300K Random Images~\cite{hendrycks2019} as a
single PyTorch tensor ($\approx$3.5\,GB) exhausted GPU
memory during energy fine-tuning.
We replaced \texttt{TensorDataset} with a custom dataset
using NumPy memory-mapped I/O (\texttt{mmap\_mode=`r'}),
so only the current mini-batch is loaded into RAM.

\noindent\textbf{Baseline reproduction.}
We initially compared against a small two-layer CNN
baseline, which artificially inflated our relative gains.
Reproducing the correct WRN-40-2 required 100 epochs of
SGD with cosine learning-rate decay---confirming the
original paper's numbers and enabling a fair comparison.

\noindent\textbf{Weak-signal contamination.}
A fixed $\alpha\!=\!0.5$ blend of the near-random energy
(AUROC~$\approx$0.51) and the strong OOD MLP
(AUROC~0.98) degraded Far-OOD performance to 0.94.
Grid-searching $\alpha$ resolved this: $\alpha^*\!=\!0.0$
correctly discards the near-random signal entirely.

%% ── 4. EXPERIMENTS AND RESULTS ──────────────────────────────
\section{Experiments and Results}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\noindent\textbf{Datasets.}
CIFAR-10~\cite{krizhevsky2009} (50\,000 train / 10\,000
test, 10 classes) is in-distribution.
Near-OOD is CIFAR-100~\cite{krizhevsky2009} (10\,000 test
images, 100 natural-image classes)---visually similar to
CIFAR-10, hence the harder evaluation.
Far-OOD is SVHN~\cite{netzer2011} (26\,032 test images of
street-view digits), which is structurally dissimilar.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics.}
Following~\cite{liu2020,hendrycks2017}: AUROC~($\uparrow$),
AUPR~($\uparrow$), and FPR@95TPR~($\downarrow$).

\noindent\textbf{Baselines.}
We reproduce the pre-trained energy score and the
energy-fine-tuned variant from~\cite{liu2020}, both using
WideResNet-40-2 trained on CIFAR-10 for 100 epochs with
SGD, cosine-decay LR schedule, and batch size 128.

\noindent\textbf{Baseline reproduction.}
Our reproduced WRN-40-2 energy scores match~\cite{liu2020}:
AUROC 0.934 on SVHN and 0.859 on CIFAR-100, confirming
our implementation's correctness.

\noindent\textbf{Far-OOD (SVHN).}
The Latent MLP achieves AUROC 0.980 and FPR@95 10.0\%,
improving the energy baseline by $+$4.6 AUROC points and
cutting false alarms at 95\% recall by 15.8 percentage
points.
SVHN digit images are structurally dissimilar to CIFAR-10
objects; the VQ-VAE codebook---built from natural patches---
poorly encodes digit patterns, producing anomalous latents.

\noindent\textbf{Near-OOD (CIFAR-100).}
The energy baseline leads decisively (0.859 vs.\ 0.704
AUROC).
CIFAR-100 shares CIFAR-10's visual distribution; the VQ-VAE
codebook describes both equally well since they contain the
same natural-image texture types.
The discriminative WRN-40-2, with 40 supervised layers,
captures semantic differences that the reconstruction-driven
encoder ignores.

\noindent\textbf{Effect of energy fine-tuning.}
Fine-tuning WRN-40-2 on 300K random images~\cite{hendrycks2019}
\emph{degraded} AUROC by $-$15 points on SVHN and $-$12
on CIFAR-100.
The proxy OOD distribution (random internet crops) does not
match real test OOD; the model overfits the proxy,
highlighting Outlier Exposure's sensitivity to auxiliary
data quality.

\noindent\textbf{Latent energy model failure.}
The Latent Energy model achieves AUROC 0.511--0.533, near
random.
Gaussian noise pseudo-OOD teaches the model to separate
``CIFAR-10 codes vs.\ Gaussian noise'' rather than
``in-distribution vs.\ real OOD codes.'' Real OOD latents
look nothing like Gaussian noise in VQ-VAE space.

\noindent\textbf{Combined score ($\alpha^*$).}
Grid search yields $\alpha^*\!=\!0.0$ on both datasets.
Blending the 0.51-AUROC energy score with the 0.98-AUROC
MLP at fixed $\alpha\!=\!0.5$ degrades performance to 0.94;
$\alpha^*\!=\!0.0$ correctly discards the weak signal and
recovers full MLP performance.

%% ── 5. CONCLUSIONS ──────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We demonstrated that the VQ-VAE latent space provides a
powerful OOD signal for visually distinct distributions:
our Latent MLP surpasses the energy baseline by
4.6 AUROC points on SVHN and halves FPR@95 from 25.8\% to
10.0\%.
For near-OOD data the discriminative energy baseline retains
a clear advantage, showing that reconstruction-driven
features alone are insufficient for datasets that share
visual structure with the training distribution.

Three limitations emerged from this work.
First, the supervised OOD MLP uses labels from the same
datasets evaluated at test time---a \textbf{data leakage}
issue we identified during evaluation.
A sound remedy is a few-shot protocol: collect a small
labeled set from a \emph{disjoint} auxiliary OOD source
and evaluate zero-shot on CIFAR-100 and SVHN.
Second, Gaussian noise is an inadequate pseudo-OOD proxy;
CutMix-based latent perturbations or codebook-index
shuffling would produce harder, more realistic negatives.
Third, energy fine-tuning degraded performance because the
proxy OOD distribution did not align with test OOD---a
reminder that Outlier Exposure requires careful selection
of auxiliary data.
