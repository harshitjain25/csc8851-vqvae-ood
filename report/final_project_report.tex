% ── Abstract ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Deep neural networks produce overconfident predictions on
inputs far outside their training distribution---a critical
failure in safety-sensitive domains.
We address out-of-distribution (OOD) detection by moving
the energy-based scoring function from a discriminative
classifier into the discrete latent space of a
Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) trained
exclusively on in-distribution data.
An unsupervised energy model trained on VQ-VAE latent codes
with a contrastive hinge loss requires no OOD labels at
training time.
A supervised MLP in the same latent space provides an
upper-bound comparison.
On CIFAR-10 (in-distribution), CIFAR-100 (near-OOD), and
SVHN (far-OOD), our Latent MLP achieves AUROC~0.980 on
SVHN---surpassing the energy baseline by 4.6 points and
reducing FPR@95 from 25.8\,\% to 10.0\,\%.
On the harder near-OOD task the energy baseline retains a
15-point AUROC advantage, motivating richer pseudo-OOD
strategies as future work.
Code: \url{https://github.com/harshitjain25/csc8851-vqvae-ood}.
\end{abstract}

% ── 1. Introduction ──────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}

Deep neural networks achieve remarkable accuracy on standard
benchmarks, yet they suffer from a systematic overconfidence
problem: they assign high-confidence predictions to inputs
that bear no resemblance to their training
data~\cite{nguyen2015deep}.
A classifier trained on CIFAR-10 objects may confidently
label a street-view digit image as ``automobile,'' or return
a confident class label for pure Gaussian noise.
In autonomous driving, clinical decision-support, and
industrial inspection, such silent failures are
unacceptable---making out-of-distribution (OOD) detection
a critical capability for deploying deep models safely.

\textbf{Problem formulation.}
Let $p_{\mathrm{in}}(x)$ be the training distribution.
At inference, given input $x$, we decide whether
$x\!\sim\!p_{\mathrm{in}}$ or $x\!\sim\!p_{\mathrm{out}}$
via a scoring function $s:\mathcal{X}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}$
and threshold~$\tau$:
\begin{equation}
  g(x;\tau) = \mathbf{1}[s(x)>\tau].
  \label{eq:decision}
\end{equation}
The goal is to choose $s$ so that in-distribution inputs
score low and OOD inputs score high.

\textbf{Limitations of existing methods.}
The maximum softmax probability~\cite{hendrycks2017baseline}
is a popular baseline but is overconfident because softmax
normalisation forces probability mass onto a known class
even for unfamiliar inputs.
Liu~\etal~\cite{liu2020energy} showed that the free energy
from raw logits,
\begin{equation}
  E(x;f) = -T\cdot\log\!\sum_{i=1}^{K} e^{f_i(x)/T},
  \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
is better calibrated and correlates with the log-partition
function of the data distribution.
Yet this energy originates from a classifier optimised for
\emph{class separability}: it measures how well an input
fits the known class labels, not whether it resembles the
overall visual structure of in-distribution images.

\textbf{Our approach.}
We hypothesise that moving the energy surface into a
\emph{generative} latent space better captures distributional
familiarity.
A VQ-VAE~\cite{vqvae} trained solely on CIFAR-10 builds a
discrete visual vocabulary from reconstruction loss alone;
OOD images forced through the same encoder produce latent
codes that ``look unfamiliar'' because the codebook was
never exposed to those visual patterns.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item We faithfully reproduce the WRN-40-2 energy
        detector of Liu~\etal~\cite{liu2020energy} and
        confirm their numbers on CIFAR-100 and SVHN.
  \item We train an \emph{unsupervised} latent energy model
        on VQ-VAE embeddings via a contrastive hinge loss,
        requiring no OOD labels.
  \item We train a supervised latent MLP as an upper-bound
        comparison and acknowledge its data-leakage
        limitation.
  \item We show that a grid-searched blend weight $\alpha^*$
        correctly discards a near-random signal, preventing
        it from contaminating a strong detector.
\end{enumerate}

% ── 2. Related Work ──────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Softmax confidence.}
Hendrycks and Gimpel~\cite{hendrycks2017baseline} proposed
the maximum softmax probability as an OOD score.
It serves as a strong initial baseline, but the
normalisation constraint makes scores unreliable for inputs
that differ substantially from the training manifold.

\textbf{ODIN.}
Liang~\etal~\cite{liang2018odin} widen the in/OOD gap using
temperature scaling and gradient-based input perturbations.
Both hyperparameters must be re-tuned for each OOD dataset,
limiting generalisation at deployment.

\textbf{Mahalanobis distance.}
Lee~\etal~\cite{lee2018mahalanobis} fit class-conditional
Gaussians to penultimate-layer features and use the
Mahalanobis distance to the nearest class mean as an OOD
score.
The approach is effective but assumes Gaussian feature
distributions and must store per-class statistics.

\textbf{Outlier Exposure.}
Hendrycks~\etal~\cite{hendrycks2019oe} demonstrated that
fine-tuning a classifier on an auxiliary OOD dataset---
encouraging uniform predictions on those examples---can
significantly improve detection.
Our supervised latent MLP is inspired by this idea but
applies it in VQ-VAE latent space.
Both methods share the same risk: if the auxiliary
distribution does not match real test OOD data, the model
overfits the proxy.

\textbf{Energy-based OOD.}
Liu~\etal~\cite{liu2020energy} provided the theoretical
grounding for Eq.~\eqref{eq:energy} as an OOD score,
showing it avoids the overconfidence bias of softmax.
Our work retains this energy formulation but relocates it
from a discriminative classifier to a generative latent
space.

\textbf{VQ-VAE.}
Van~den~Oord~\etal~\cite{vqvae} introduced the
Vector-Quantized VAE, which encodes images as sequences of
discrete tokens from a learned codebook.
Because the codebook is shaped entirely by the reconstruction
objective on in-distribution data, it provides a signal for
distributional familiarity that is orthogonal to class
labels.

\subsection{VQ-VAE Latent Encoding}

The VQ-VAE (trained by Parsh Jadon) maps each $32{\times}32$
image through encoder $E_\theta$, codebook
$\mathcal{C}=\{\mathbf{e}_k\}_{k=1}^{K}$ ($K\!=\!512$), and
decoder $D_\phi$.
The continuous embedding
$\mathbf{z}_e\!=\!E_\theta(x)\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{8\times8\times64}$
is quantised to the nearest entry:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z}_q = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{e}_k\in\mathcal{C}}
    \|\mathbf{z}_e-\mathbf{e}_k\|_2.
  \label{eq:quant}
\end{equation}
Training minimises the three-term objective
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vq}} =
  \underbrace{\|x{-}D_\phi(\mathbf{z}_q)\|^2}_{\text{recon.}}
  +\underbrace{\|\mathrm{sg}[\mathbf{z}_e]{-}\mathbf{e}_k\|^2}_{\text{codebook}}
  +\beta\underbrace{\|\mathbf{z}_e{-}\mathrm{sg}[\mathbf{e}_k]\|^2}_{\text{commit.}},
  \label{eq:vqloss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{sg}[\cdot]$ is stop-gradient and
$\beta\!=\!0.25$.
The model is trained with Adam (lr$\!=\!10^{-3}$), batch
size~128, for 10 epochs on the 50\,000-image CIFAR-10
training set.
Because the codebook is shaped solely by CIFAR-10
reconstruction, it encodes only in-distribution visual
patterns.
Flattening the $8{\times}8{\times}64$ quantised tensor yields
$\mathbf{z}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{4096}$.

\subsection{Latent Energy Model}

$E_{\mathrm{lat}}$ is a three-layer MLP (256 hidden units,
ReLU) mapping $\mathbb{R}^{4096}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}$.
It is trained \emph{only} on CIFAR-10 latents; Gaussian
noise $\mathbf{z}_n\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$
acts as cheap pseudo-OOD, requiring no real OOD data.
A squared hinge loss with margin targets
$m_{\mathrm{in}}\!=\!-10$ and $m_{\mathrm{out}}\!=\!-5$
enforces separation:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{in}}  &=
    \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}\sim p_{\mathrm{ID}}}\!
    \bigl[\max(0,E_{\mathrm{lat}}(\mathbf{z})-m_{\mathrm{in}})^2\bigr],
    \label{eq:lin}\\
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{out}} &=
    \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}\sim\mathcal{N}}\!
    \bigl[\max(0,m_{\mathrm{out}}-E_{\mathrm{lat}}(\mathbf{z}))^2\bigr],
    \label{eq:lout}
\end{align}
with total loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{energy}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{in}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{out}}$.
The hinge sets the gradient to zero once a sample is on the
correct side of its margin, preventing energy collapse and
bounding gradients.
Training uses Adam (lr$\!=\!10^{-3}$) for 10 epochs.

\subsection{Supervised OOD MLP}

For an upper-bound comparison we train a binary MLP with
the same architecture, optimised with binary cross-entropy:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MLP}} = -\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{z},y)}\!
  \bigl[y\log\hat{y}+(1{-}y)\log(1{-}\hat{y})\bigr],
  \label{eq:bce}
\end{equation}
where $y\!=\!0$ for CIFAR-10 latents and $y\!=\!1$ for
CIFAR-100 / SVHN latents.

\textbf{Data-leakage caveat.}
We found that this setup constitutes \emph{data leakage}:
the supervised MLP uses labeled samples from the same
datasets evaluated at test time.
A principled design would restrict OOD supervision to a
small labeled set from a \emph{disjoint} auxiliary source,
then evaluate zero-shot on the target datasets.
We retain this setting to measure an upper bound but
flag it explicitly as a limitation
(Section~\ref{sec:conclusions}).

\subsection{Combined Score and Threshold}

Both raw scores are min-max normalised using CIFAR-10
statistics:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{s} = (s-s_{\min})\,/\,(s_{\max}-s_{\min}).
  \label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}
The combined score is a weighted average,
\begin{equation}
  s_{\mathrm{comb}}(\mathbf{z}) =
    \alpha\,\tilde{E}_{\mathrm{lat}}(\mathbf{z})
    +(1{-}\alpha)\,\tilde{y}(\mathbf{z}),
  \label{eq:comb}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha^*$ is chosen by grid search over
$\{0.0,0.1,\ldots,1.0\}$ on a held-out validation split
and applied to a disjoint test split.
The detection threshold $\tau$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:decision}
is fixed so that 95\,\% of in-distribution test samples are
correctly accepted (TPR\,=\,0.95).

\subsection{Implementation Challenges}

Several engineering difficulties arose during development.

\noindent\textbf{Memory-mapped OOD data.}
Loading all 300K Random Images~\cite{hendrycks2019oe} as a
single PyTorch tensor ($\approx$3.5\,GB) exhausted GPU
memory during energy fine-tuning.
We resolved this by replacing \texttt{TensorDataset} with a
custom dataset that uses NumPy memory-mapped I/O
(\texttt{mmap\_mode=`r'}), loading only the current batch
into RAM at each training step.

\noindent\textbf{Baseline reproduction.}
We initially compared our methods against a small two-layer
CNN baseline, which artificially inflated our relative
gains.
Reproducing the correct WRN-40-2 baseline required 100
epochs of SGD training with cosine learning-rate
decay---confirming the original paper's numbers and enabling
a fair comparison.

\noindent\textbf{Weak-signal contamination.}
A fixed $\alpha\!=\!0.5$ blend of the latent energy
(AUROC~$\approx$\,0.51, near random) and the OOD MLP
(AUROC~0.98) degraded Far-OOD performance from 0.98 to 0.94.
Grid-searching $\alpha$ on a held-out validation partition
resolved this: the optimal $\alpha^*\!=\!0.0$ correctly
discards the near-random energy signal.

% ── 4. Experiments and Results ───────────────────────────────
\section{Experiments and Results}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\noindent\textbf{Datasets.}
CIFAR-10~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning} (50\,000 train /
10\,000 test, 10 classes) is our in-distribution set.
Near-OOD is CIFAR-100~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning}
(10\,000 test images, 100 natural-image classes)---visually
similar to CIFAR-10 and therefore the harder evaluation.
Far-OOD is SVHN~\cite{netzer2011svhn} (26\,032 test images
of street-view digit photographs), which is structurally
dissimilar to CIFAR-10.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics.}
Following~\cite{liu2020energy,hendrycks2017baseline}: AUROC
($\uparrow$), AUPR ($\uparrow$), and FPR@95TPR ($\downarrow$).

\noindent\textbf{Baselines.}
We reproduce the pre-trained energy score and the
energy-fine-tuned variant from Liu~\etal~\cite{liu2020energy},
both using WideResNet-40-2 trained on CIFAR-10 for
100~epochs with SGD, cosine-decay learning rate schedule,
and batch size~128.

\noindent\textbf{Baseline reproduction.}
Our reproduced WRN-40-2 energy scores match the numbers
reported in~\cite{liu2020energy}: AUROC~0.934 on SVHN and
0.859 on CIFAR-100, confirming the correctness of our
implementation.

\noindent\textbf{Far-OOD (SVHN).}
The Latent MLP achieves AUROC~0.980 and FPR@95~10.0\%,
improving the energy baseline by $+$4.6~AUROC points and
cutting false alarms by 15.8 percentage points at 95\,\%
recall.
SVHN digit images are structurally dissimilar to CIFAR-10
natural objects; the VQ-VAE codebook---built from natural
image patches---poorly encodes digit patterns, producing
clearly anomalous latent codes.

\noindent\textbf{Near-OOD (CIFAR-100).}
The energy baseline leads decisively (0.859 vs.\ 0.704
AUROC).
CIFAR-100 shares CIFAR-10's visual distribution; the VQ-VAE
codebook describes both datasets equally well because they
contain the same types of natural-image textures.
The discriminative WRN-40-2, with 40 supervised layers,
captures semantic differences that the reconstruction-driven
encoder ignores.

\noindent\textbf{Effect of energy fine-tuning.}
Fine-tuning WRN-40-2 on 300K random internet images
\cite{hendrycks2019oe} \emph{degraded} AUROC by $-$15
points on SVHN and $-$12 points on CIFAR-100.
The proxy OOD distribution (random internet crops) does not
resemble real test OOD distributions; the model overfits the
proxy, highlighting the sensitivity of Outlier Exposure to
the choice of auxiliary data.

\noindent\textbf{Latent energy model failure.}
The Latent Energy model achieves AUROC~0.511--0.533, near
random.
Gaussian noise pseudo-OOD teaches the model to separate
``CIFAR-10 codes vs.\ Gaussian noise'' rather than
``in-distribution vs.\ real OOD codes''; real OOD latents
look nothing like Gaussian noise in the VQ-VAE space.

\noindent\textbf{Combined score ($\alpha^*$).}
Grid search yields $\alpha^*\!=\!0.0$ on both datasets.
Blending the 0.51-AUROC energy score with the 0.98-AUROC
MLP at fixed $\alpha\!=\!0.5$ degrades performance to 0.94;
$\alpha^*\!=\!0.0$ correctly discards the weak signal and
recovers MLP performance.

% ── 5. Conclusions ───────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We demonstrated that the VQ-VAE latent space provides a
powerful OOD signal for visually distinct distributions:
our Latent MLP surpasses the energy baseline by
4.6~AUROC points on SVHN and halves FPR@95 from 25.8\,\% to
10.0\,\%.
For near-OOD data the discriminative energy baseline retains
a clear advantage, showing that reconstruction-driven
features alone are insufficient to capture semantic
differences between similar-looking datasets.

Three limitations emerged from this work.
First, the supervised OOD MLP uses labels from the same
datasets evaluated at test time---a \textbf{data-leakage}
issue we identified during evaluation.
A sound remedy is a few-shot protocol: collect a small
labeled set from a \emph{disjoint} auxiliary OOD source,
train the MLP on it, then evaluate zero-shot.
Second, Gaussian noise is an inadequate pseudo-OOD proxy
for the energy model; CutMix-based latent perturbations or
codebook-index shuffling would produce harder, more
realistic negatives.
Third, energy fine-tuning degraded performance because the
proxy OOD distribution did not align with the test OOD
distributions---a reminder that Outlier Exposure requires
careful auxiliary data selection.
