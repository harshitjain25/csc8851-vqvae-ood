% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Deep neural networks produce overconfident predictions on
inputs far outside their training distribution---a critical
failure in safety-sensitive domains.
We address out-of-distribution (OOD) detection by moving
the energy-based scoring function from a discriminative
classifier into the discrete latent space of a
Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) trained
exclusively on in-distribution data.
An unsupervised energy model trained on VQ-VAE latent codes
with a contrastive hinge loss requires no OOD labels.
A supervised MLP in the same space provides an upper-bound
comparison.
On CIFAR-10 (in-distribution), CIFAR-100 (near-OOD), and
SVHN (far-OOD), our Latent MLP achieves AUROC~0.980 on
SVHN---surpassing the energy baseline by 4.6 points and
reducing FPR@95 from 25.8\% to 10.0\%.
On the harder near-OOD task the energy baseline retains a
15-point AUROC advantage, motivating richer pseudo-OOD
strategies as future work.
Code: \url{https://github.com/harshitjain25/csc8851-vqvae-ood}.
\end{abstract}

% ── 1. Introduction ───────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}

Deep neural networks are known to assign high confidence to
inputs that bear no resemblance to their training
data~\cite{nguyen2015deep}.
A model trained on everyday objects may confidently label a
medical scan as ``automobile.''
In autonomous driving, medical imaging, and industrial
inspection, such silent failures are unacceptable---which
is why out-of-distribution (OOD) detection has become an
active research area.

\textbf{Problem formulation.}
Let $p_{\mathrm{in}}(x)$ be the training distribution.
At inference, given input $x$, we decide whether
$x\!\sim\!p_{\mathrm{in}}$ or $x\!\sim\!p_{\mathrm{out}}$
via a scoring function $s:\mathcal{X}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}$
and threshold $\tau$:
\begin{equation}
  g(x;\tau) = \mathbf{1}[s(x)>\tau].
  \label{eq:decision}
\end{equation}
The goal is to choose $s$ so that in-distribution inputs
score low and OOD inputs score high, maximising the area
under the ROC curve.

\textbf{Limitations of existing methods.}
The maximum softmax probability~\cite{hendrycks2017baseline}
is a popular baseline but is notoriously overconfident even
for unfamiliar inputs.
Liu~et~al.~\cite{liu2020energy} showed that the free energy
derived from raw logits,
\begin{equation}
  E(x;f) = -T\cdot\log\!\sum_{i=1}^{K} e^{f_i(x)/T},
  \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
is better calibrated because it correlates with the
log-partition function of the data distribution.
Yet this energy still originates from a classifier
optimised for \emph{class separability}; it measures how
well an input fits the known classes, not whether it
resembles the overall visual structure of the training
distribution.

\textbf{Our approach.}
We ask whether computing energy in a \emph{generative}
latent space better captures distributional familiarity.
A VQ-VAE~\cite{vqvae} trained solely on CIFAR-10 builds a
discrete visual vocabulary for that dataset; OOD images
forced through the same encoder produce ``unfamiliar''
latent codes that a lightweight network can detect.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item We faithfully reproduce the WRN-40-2 energy
    detector of Liu~et~al.~\cite{liu2020energy} and confirm
    their reported numbers.
  \item We train an \emph{unsupervised} latent energy model
    on VQ-VAE embeddings via a contrastive hinge loss,
    requiring no OOD labels.
  \item We train a supervised latent MLP as an upper-bound
    and explicitly acknowledge its data-leakage limitation.
  \item We demonstrate that blending a near-random signal
    with a strong one degrades performance, and that a
    grid-searched blend weight $\alpha^*$ correctly
    discards the weak signal.
\end{enumerate}

% ── 2. Related Work ───────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Softmax confidence.}
Hendrycks and Gimpel~\cite{hendrycks2017baseline} proposed
using maximum softmax probability as a simple OOD score.
It works surprisingly often but is fundamentally limited by
softmax normalisation, which forces confidently-sounding
outputs regardless of input familiarity.

\textbf{ODIN.}
Liang~et~al.~\cite{liang2018odin} widened the in/OOD score
gap using temperature scaling and gradient-based input
perturbations.
Both hyperparameters must be tuned per OOD dataset, limiting
out-of-the-box generalisation.

\textbf{Mahalanobis distance.}
Lee~et~al.~\cite{lee2018mahalanobis} fit class-conditional
Gaussians to penultimate-layer features and use Mahalanobis
distance as the OOD score.
The approach is effective but assumes Gaussian feature
distributions and requires storing per-class statistics.

\textbf{Outlier Exposure.}
Hendrycks~et~al.~\cite{hendrycks2019oe} showed that
fine-tuning a classifier on auxiliary OOD data significantly
improves detection.
Our supervised latent MLP is inspired by this idea but
operates in VQ-VAE latent space and inherits the same
risk: proxy OOD data that does not match real test
distributions causes the model to overfit the proxy.

\textbf{Energy-based OOD.}
Liu~et~al.~\cite{liu2020energy} provided a theoretical
grounding for energy-based detection via the
log-partition function, avoiding the overconfidence bias of
softmax.
Our work retains this energy formulation but moves the
scoring surface from a discriminative classifier to a
generative latent space.

\textbf{VQ-VAE.}
Van den~Oord~et~al.~\cite{vqvae} introduced the
Vector-Quantized VAE, which encodes images as sequences of
discrete tokens from a learned codebook.
Because the codebook is shaped entirely by the reconstruction
objective on in-distribution data, it is a natural signal
for distributional familiarity---distinct from any class
label information.

\subsection{VQ-VAE Latent Encoding}

The VQ-VAE (trained by Parsh Jadon) consists of encoder
$E_\theta$, codebook
$\mathcal{C}=\{\mathbf{e}_k\}_{k=1}^{K}$ ($K\!=\!512$),
and decoder $D_\phi$.
The continuous embedding
$\mathbf{z}_e\!=\!E_\theta(x)\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{8\times8\times64}$
is quantised to the nearest codebook entry,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z}_q = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{e}_k\in\mathcal{C}}
    \|\mathbf{z}_e-\mathbf{e}_k\|_2,
  \label{eq:quant}
\end{equation}
using the three-term objective
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vq}} =
  \underbrace{\|x{-}D_\phi(\mathbf{z}_q)\|^2}_{\text{reconstruction}}
  +\underbrace{\|\mathrm{sg}[\mathbf{z}_e]{-}\mathbf{e}_k\|^2}_{\text{codebook}}
  +\beta\underbrace{\|\mathbf{z}_e{-}\mathrm{sg}[\mathbf{e}_k]\|^2}_{\text{commitment}},
  \label{eq:vqloss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{sg}[\cdot]$ is the stop-gradient operator and
$\beta\!=\!0.25$.
The VQ-VAE trains only on CIFAR-10, so $\mathcal{C}$
encodes exclusively in-distribution visual patterns.
Flattening the $8{\times}8{\times}64$ quantised tensor yields
$\mathbf{z}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{4096}$.

\subsection{Latent Energy Model}

$E_{\mathrm{lat}}$ is a three-layer MLP (256 hidden units,
ReLU) mapping $\mathbb{R}^{4096}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}$.
It is trained on CIFAR-10 latents only, with Gaussian noise
$\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{noise}}\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$
serving as pseudo-OOD.
A squared hinge loss with margin targets
$m_{\mathrm{in}}\!=\!-10$ and $m_{\mathrm{out}}\!=\!-5$
enforces separation:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{in}}  &=
    \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}\sim p_{\mathrm{ID}}}\!
    \bigl[\max(0,\,E_{\mathrm{lat}}(\mathbf{z})-m_{\mathrm{in}})^2\bigr],
    \label{eq:lin}\\
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{out}} &=
    \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}\sim\mathcal{N}}\!
    \bigl[\max(0,\,m_{\mathrm{out}}-E_{\mathrm{lat}}(\mathbf{z}))^2\bigr],
    \label{eq:lout}
\end{align}
with
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{energy}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{in}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{out}}$.
The hinge prevents gradient updates once a sample is already
on the correct side of its margin, stabilising training.
No real OOD images are used at any point.

\subsection{Supervised OOD MLP}

For an upper-bound comparison we train a binary MLP with the
same architecture, optimised with binary cross-entropy:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MLP}} = -\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{z},y)}\!
  \bigl[y\log\hat{y}+(1{-}y)\log(1{-}\hat{y})\bigr],
  \label{eq:bce}
\end{equation}
where $y\!=\!0$ for CIFAR-10 latents and $y\!=\!1$ for
CIFAR-100 / SVHN latents.

\textbf{Data-leakage caveat.}
As identified during our poster evaluation, this setup
constitutes \emph{data leakage}: the supervised MLP is
trained on labeled samples drawn from the same datasets
used for final evaluation.
A sound protocol would restrict OOD supervision to a small
few-shot sample from a \emph{disjoint} auxiliary source and
evaluate zero-shot on CIFAR-100 and SVHN.
We retain this setting to quantify an upper bound but
explicitly flag it as a limitation in Section~\ref{sec:conclusions}.

\subsection{Combined Score and Threshold}

Both raw scores are min-max normalised using CIFAR-10
statistics before blending:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{s} = (s-s_{\min})\,/\,(s_{\max}-s_{\min}).
  \label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}
The combined detection score is a weighted average,
\begin{equation}
  s_{\mathrm{comb}}(\mathbf{z}) =
    \alpha\,\tilde{E}_{\mathrm{lat}}(\mathbf{z})
    +(1{-}\alpha)\,\tilde{y}(\mathbf{z}),
  \label{eq:comb}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha^*$ is selected by grid search over
$\{0.0,0.1,\ldots,1.0\}$ on a held-out validation split
and applied to a disjoint test split.
The detection threshold $\tau$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:decision}
is set so that 95\% of in-distribution test samples are
correctly accepted (TPR\,=\,0.95).

% ── 4. Experiments and Results ────────────────────────────────
\section{Experiments and Results}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\textbf{Datasets.}
CIFAR-10~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning} (50\,000 train /
10\,000 test, 10 classes) serves as in-distribution.
Near-OOD is CIFAR-100~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning}
(10\,000 test images, 100 natural-image classes)---visually
similar to CIFAR-10 and therefore the harder evaluation.
Far-OOD is SVHN~\cite{netzer2011svhn} (26\,032 test images
of street-view digits), which is structurally dissimilar to
CIFAR-10.

\textbf{Metrics.}
Following~\cite{liu2020energy,hendrycks2017baseline}, we
report AUROC ($\uparrow$), AUPR ($\uparrow$), and
FPR@95TPR ($\downarrow$).

\textbf{Baselines.}
We reproduce the pre-trained energy score and the
energy-fine-tuned variant from Liu~et~al.~\cite{liu2020energy},
both using a WideResNet-40-2 trained on CIFAR-10 for
100~epochs with SGD, cosine-decay learning rate, and
batch size~128.

\textbf{Far-OOD (SVHN).}
The Latent MLP achieves AUROC~0.980 and FPR@95~10.0\%,
improving the energy baseline by $+$4.6 AUROC points and
reducing false alarms at 95\% recall by 15.8 percentage
points.
SVHN digit images are structurally dissimilar to CIFAR-10
natural images; the VQ-VAE codebook---trained on natural
patches---poorly encodes digit patterns, producing clearly
anomalous latent codes that the MLP exploits.

\textbf{Near-OOD (CIFAR-100).}
The energy baseline retains a decisive lead
(0.859 vs.\ 0.704 AUROC).
CIFAR-100 shares CIFAR-10's visual distribution; the VQ-VAE
codebook describes both datasets equally well because they
contain the same types of natural-image textures.
The discriminative WRN-40-2 captures semantic differences
that the generative model ignores.

\textbf{Effect of energy fine-tuning.}
Fine-tuning WRN-40-2 on 300K random internet images
\cite{hendrycks2019oe} \emph{degraded} AUROC by
$-$15 points on SVHN and $-$12 points on CIFAR-100.
The proxy OOD distribution (random crops) does not resemble
real test OOD distributions, causing the model to overfit
the proxy---an instance of the Outlier Exposure sensitivity
noted in~\cite{hendrycks2019oe}.

\textbf{Latent energy model failure.}
The Latent Energy model achieves AUROC~0.511--0.533,
near random.
Gaussian noise pseudo-OOD trains the model to separate
``CIFAR-10 latents vs.\ Gaussian noise'' rather than
``in-distribution vs.\ real OOD codes,'' which look nothing
like Gaussian noise in the VQ-VAE latent space.

\textbf{Combined score ($\alpha^*$).}
Grid search yields $\alpha^*\!=\!0.0$ on both datasets,
reducing the combined score to the MLP alone.
Blending a near-random energy signal (AUROC~0.51) with a
strong MLP (AUROC~0.98) degrades performance, confirming
that a weak signal should be discarded rather than averaged.

% ── 5. Conclusions ────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

We demonstrated that the VQ-VAE latent space provides a
powerful OOD signal for visually distinct distributions:
our Latent MLP surpasses the energy baseline by
4.6~AUROC points on SVHN and halves FPR@95 from
25.8\% to 10.0\%.
For near-OOD data the discriminative energy baseline
retains a clear advantage, highlighting the need for
encoders that capture semantic rather than purely visual
similarity.

Two limitations are critical for future work.
First, the supervised OOD MLP is trained on the exact
datasets used for evaluation---a \textbf{data leakage}
issue identified at our poster presentation.
A principled remedy is a few-shot protocol: use a small
labeled set from a disjoint auxiliary OOD source for
supervision and evaluate zero-shot on CIFAR-100 and SVHN.
Second, Gaussian noise is an inadequate pseudo-OOD proxy.
CutMix-based latent perturbations or codebook-index
shuffling would produce harder, more realistic negatives
and likely improve near-OOD performance.
